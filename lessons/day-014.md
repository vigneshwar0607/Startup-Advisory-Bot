## 1. Today in one line
Consolidating startup governance, compliance, and investor readiness into a billable CA service.

## 2. The concept
The last two weeks covered the lifecycle of a startup from a CA's perspective. The core concept is **Investor Readiness**. 

A startup is not just a business; it is an asset being prepared for sale (equity). Therefore, the "product" you help the founder build is the company's legal and financial structure. This involves:
*   **Governance:** Moving from a loose Founders' Agreement to a formal Shareholders' Agreement (SHA) with clauses like Liquidation Preference and Anti-Dilution.
*   **Compliance:** Shifting from "annual filing" to a "compliance calendar" that tracks GST, TDS, and ROC deadlines monthly to avoid the "clean-up cost" during due diligence.
*   **Asset Protection:** Ensuring Intellectual Property (IP) is not just created but legally owned by the entity.
*   **Transparency:** Maintaining a Virtual Data Room (VDR) and a monthly Management Information System (MIS) so the founder can answer investor questions instantly.

## 5. Watch-outs
*   **The "Founder-Owner" Trap:** Founders often think they own the IP because they "thought of it." If there is no assignment agreement, the company owns nothing, and the valuation drops to zero.
*   **The DSC Trap:** Expired Digital Signature Certificates (DSCs) of directors often delay urgent ROC filings or funding rounds.
*   **The "Lazy" Accounting Trap:** Recording "Director's Salary" as "Drawings" in a Private Limited company. This creates massive tax and compliance headaches later.

## 8. Digital Rupee today
I found no new RBI press releases or technical updates regarding the Digital Rupee (e₹) in the last 30 days (as of 27 November 2026).

**e₹ vs Stablecoins vs Crypto — and the VDA tax angle:**
*   **e₹ (CBDC):** It is sovereign currency in digital form. It is not a Virtual Digital Asset (VDA). No 30% tax on "gains" because its value is always 1:1 with cash.
*   **Stablecoins (e.g., USDT, USDC):** These are private tokens pegged to a fiat currency. In India, these are classified as VDAs.
*   **Crypto (e.g., BTC, ETH):** Decentralized assets. These are VDAs.
*   **Tax Angle:** Under Section 115BBH of the Income-tax Act, 1961 (verify equivalent in IT Act, 2025), VDA gains are taxed at 30% with no set-off of losses. e₹ transactions are treated like cash/bank transfers and do not attract VDA tax or 1% TDS under Section 194S.

**Why it matters:** Startups using e₹ for B2B payments avoid the complex TDS and tax reporting associated with crypto-assets.
