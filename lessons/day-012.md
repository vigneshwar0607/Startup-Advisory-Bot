# Startup Advisory — Day 12: Digital Rupee (e₹) 101

_2026-11-25_

## 1. Today in one line
Digital Rupee (e₹) is sovereign cash in digital token form issued by RBI, serving as legal tender without commercial bank credit risk.

## 2. The concept
The Digital Rupee (e₹) is India's Central Bank Digital Currency (CBDC), issued by the Reserve Bank of India (RBI) under the amended Reserve Bank of India Act, 1934. The Finance Act, 2022 amended Section 2 and Section 22 of the RBI Act to explicitly grant RBI authority to issue digital banknotes as legal tender.

CBDC comes in two distinct variants:
1. Wholesale Digital Rupee (e₹-W): Restricted to select financial institutions. It is used for inter-bank settlement, secondary market transactions in Government Securities (G-Secs), and call money market operations. Settling transactions via e₹-W eliminates clearing corporation counterparty risk and reduces settlement cycles.
2. Retail Digital Rupee (e₹-R): Available for public use by individuals, merchants, and startups. Distributed via digital wallets offered by participating commercial banks, e₹-R enables Person-to-Person (P2P) and Person-to-Merchant (P2M) transactions.

Key distinctions to understand:
- e₹ vs. UPI: UPI (Unified Payments Interface) is an instruction and payment processing system operated by NPCI that moves funds between commercial bank accounts. When you pay via UPI, money moves from Bank A to Bank B. e₹ is the digital asset itself—a direct claim on RBI's balance sheet. Transferring an e₹ token IS the settlement, requiring no inter-bank clearing.
- e₹ vs. Bank Deposits: Commercial bank deposits carry credit risk (covered by DICGC insurance up to ₹5 lakh per bank). e₹ carries zero credit or liquidity risk because it is RBI money. Crucially, e₹ does not earn interest, ensuring it does not trigger mass withdrawal from bank savings accounts (disintermediation).
- e₹ vs. Cryptocurrency / VDAs: Cryptocurrencies (like Bitcoin) and Virtual Digital Assets (VDAs) are private, unbacked digital tokens. e₹ is official legal tender backed by the sovereign reserve of India and is explicitly excluded from the definition of Virtual Digital Assets under tax laws (Section 2(47A) of Income-tax Act, 1961 / Income-tax Act, 2025 equivalent; unverified — check official source).

Current pilot developments include offline payment capabilities, UPI QR code interoperability, and programmable e₹ payouts (allowing funds to be restricted for specific use cases like central schemes or escrow payouts).

## 5. Watch-outs
1. **Confusing e₹ with VDAs**: Clients often mistake e₹ for crypto. Treating e₹ transactions under VDA tax provisions (30% tax without loss set-off, 1% TDS under Section 194S of IT Act 1961 / 2025 equivalent) is a major tax error. e₹ is standard legal tender.
2. **Reconciliation Gaps**: e₹ wallets operate on unique token IDs. Finance teams must map e₹ digital wallet statements to the general ledger, preventing unrecorded cash-equivalent float.
3. **No Interest Yield**: Storing large operational capital in e₹ wallet balance earns zero interest. Advice startups to maintain only necessary operational cash float in e₹ wallets.

e₹-W (Wholesale CBDC) is designed specifically for inter-bank settlement. In traditional inter-bank transactions or government securities trading, settlement relies on clearing corporations and central counterparty clearing mechanisms, which incur time delays and settlement risk. With e₹-W, commercial banks settle wholesale obligations using digital sovereign tokens directly on RBI's ledger in real time, eliminating settlement risk and counterparty exposure.

For a CA advising startups, understanding e₹-W clarifies how institutional banking infrastructure is evolving toward instant, zero-counterparty settlement, paving the way for programmable wholesale corporate escrow and trade finance solutions.
