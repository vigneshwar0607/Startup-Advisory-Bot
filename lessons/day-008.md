# Startup Advisory — Day 8: AoA, SHA Clauses and Statutory Registers

_2026-11-21_

## 1. Today in one line
An investor agreement is toothless against the company unless entrenched in the Articles of Association, and investor due diligence will stall if statutory registers are not fully updated.

## 2. The concept
When a startup raises institutional capital (seed or Series A), the commercial terms agreed upon in the Term Sheet are codified in a Shareholders' Agreement (SHA). However, a critical legal principle under Indian corporate law—established in landmark jurisprudence such as *V.B. Rangaraj v. V.B. Gopalakrishnan*—is that private agreements between shareholders are not binding on the company unless their terms are formally incorporated into the company's Articles of Association (AoA). 

If a founder breaches an SHA transfer restriction, the investor's only remedy is a civil suit for breach of contract against the founder; the company is legally obligated to register the transfer unless the restriction is in its AoA. Therefore, closing a round always requires amending the AoA via a special resolution under Section 14 of the Companies Act, 2013, and notifying entrenchment provisions under Section 5(6) by filing Form MGT-14 with the Registrar of Companies (RoC).

### Essential Clauses Investors Demand
1. **Affirmative Voting Rights (Reserved Matters):** Specified strategic matters (e.g., alteration of capital, incurring debt above a specified threshold, key executive hires, entering new business lines) that cannot be passed at Board or General Meetings without the affirmative vote of the investor nominee.
2. **Transfer Restrictions:**
   - *Lock-in:* Founders are typically locked in for 3 to 5 years.
   - *ROFR / ROFO:* Right of First Refusal or Right of First Offer in favor of investors before shares can be sold to third parties.
   - *Tag-Along Right:* If founders sell their stake, investors have the right to participate pro-rata on the same terms.
   - *Drag-Along Right:* Allows majority investors to compel minority shareholders (including founders) to sell their shares if an acquirer offers to buy the company.
3. **Liquidation Preference:** Defines payout priority upon a "liquidation event" (sale, merger, or winding up). Early-stage standard is "1x Non-Participating", meaning the investor receives their original investment amount first, or converts to equity to participate pro-rata with common shareholders, but not both.
4. **Anti-Dilution Protection:** Protects the investor if the company issues shares at a lower valuation in a future round (down-round). Standard institutional practice is the "Broad-Based Weighted Average" formula; founders must resist "Full Ratchet" (which resets the prior round's share price entirely to the down-round price).

### Statutory Registers
Under the Companies Act, 2013, every private limited startup must maintain statutory registers at its registered office. Missing registers represent an immediate red flag during legal due diligence.

- **Register of Members (Section 88, Form MGT-1):** Records names, addresses, allotments, and transfers.
- **Register of Debenture Holders / Security Holders (Section 88, Form MGT-2):** Essential if issuing Compulsorily Convertible Debentures (CCDs).
- **Register of Directors and Key Managerial Personnel (Section 170):** Details of appointments, cessations, and their shareholdings.
- **Register of Significant Beneficial Owners (Section 90, Form BEN-3):** Tracks individuals holding indirect or direct beneficial interest of 10% or more.
- **Register of Loans, Guarantees, Securities, and Investments (Section 186, Form MBP-2):** Even if zero, must reflect inter-company transactions.
- **Register of Contracts in which Directors are Interested (Section 189, Form MBP-4):** Documents related-party transactions, founder salaries, and advisory agreements.

## 3. Practical CA angle
- **The Service:** Corporate Governance & Investor-Readiness Advisory. Specifically: SHA review (financial and governance covenants), AoA amendment drafting, and statutory register reconstruction.
- **Typical Deliverables:** 
  1. Marked-up draft of SHA highlighting restrictive operational covenants.
  2. Altered AoA incorporating SHA terms with entrenchment clauses.
  3. Notice, Board/EGM resolutions, and Form MGT-14 filing package.
  4. Complete, indexed set of physical or digital statutory registers (Forms MGT-1, MBP-2, MBP-4, BEN-3).
- **Indicative Fee Range:** 
  - Register cleanup and reconstruction: INR 15,000 to INR 35,000.
  - AoA drafting, entrenchment, and MGT-14 filing: INR 25,000 to INR 60,000.

## 4. Worked example or checklist

### Investor Clause Defense Checklist for Founders
| Clause | Investor Objective | Recommended Founder Counter-Position |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Reserved Matters** | Veto power over company decisions | Set clear financial thresholds (e.g., debt veto only above INR 25 lakhs); exempt approved annual business budgets. |
| **Drag-Along** | Force sale to an acquirer | Add a minimum valuation floor and a minimum time buffer (e.g., cannot trigger before Year 4). |
| **Liquidation Preference** | Capital downside protection | Stick strictly to 1x Non-Participating; reject Participating preferred terms. |
| **Anti-Dilution** | Value preservation in down-rounds | Broad-Based Weighted Average only; exclude ESOP expansions and strategic partner warrants from calculation. |

### Statutory Registers Verification Checklist
| Register | Form | Statutory Timeline | Key Data Points Required |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Members** | MGT-1 | Update within 7 days of board approval | Folio number, distinct share certificate numbers, consideration paid. |
| **Directors / KMP** | Rule 17 | On appointment / change | DIN, PAN, residential address, date of appointment, shareholding details. |
| **SBO** | BEN-3 | 30 days from BEN-1 receipt | Natural person holding ultimate beneficial interest, percentage holding. |
| **Related Contracts** | MBP-4 | At subsequent board meeting | Names of bodies corporate/firms where directors have interest (Form MBP-1). |
| **Loans & Investments** | MBP-2 | Within 7 days of transaction | Date of loan/guarantee, rate of interest, principal amount, borrower details. |

## 5. Watch-outs
1. **The Unenforceability Trap:** Never assume signing the SHA is sufficient. If Form MGT-14 is not filed to amend the AoA, any restrictions on share transfers are ineffective against third parties and the company.
2. **Missing SBO Declarations:** Failure to identify Significant Beneficial Owners and maintain Register BEN-3 triggers hefty penalties under Section 90(10) and (11) on both the company and the officers in default.
3. **Overly Broad Reserved Matters:** If routine operational expenses (like hiring an engineer or spending on basic marketing) require investor sign-off, day-to-day operations will grind to a halt.
4. **Neglected MBP-4 Entries:** When founders draw salaries or rent their personal property to the startup without an MBP-4 entry and board disclosure, it surfaces as an immediate compliance default during Series A diligence.

## 7. Quick quiz
1. Which landmark legal precedent established that restrictions on share transfers in an agreement are unenforceable against the company unless incorporated into its Articles?
2. What is the standard statutory time limit to update the Register of Members (Form MGT-1) following an allotment of shares?
3. What is the difference between a ROFR and a Tag-Along right?

### Answers
1. *V.B. Rangaraj v. V.B. Gopalakrishnan* (reaffirmed across corporate jurisprudence).
2. Within 7 days from the date the Board of Directors approves the allotment or transfer.
3. A ROFR (Right of First Refusal) gives existing investors the right to purchase the selling shareholder's shares before an outsider; a Tag-Along right allows the investor to participate alongside the selling shareholder and sell their own shares to the outsider buyer.

**Concept: Offline e₹.** Offline Central Bank Digital Currency (CBDC) allows digital rupee transfers between devices without requiring active telecom or internet connectivity. The architecture uses secure hardware elements (such as embedded secure elements in smartphones or NFC-enabled smart cards) to ensure cryptographic integrity and prevent double-spending while disconnected from the central ledger.

**Relevance to CAs and Startups:** Startups operating in rural commerce, transit ticketing, or remote point-of-sale hardware must design their payment workflows around offline-capable digital fiat. For CAs, offline e₹ introduces new audit considerations: verifying periodic ledger syncs, mitigating offline double-spend disputes, and auditing asynchronous transaction reconciliations.
