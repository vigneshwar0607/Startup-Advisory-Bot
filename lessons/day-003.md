# Startup Advisory — Day 3: Entity Structuring and Investor Preferences

_2026-11-16_

## 1. Today in one line
Institutional investors fund corporate equity, not partnership shares: incorporate a Private Limited Company if external fundraising or ESOPs are on the roadmap.

## 2. The concept
Founders frequently ask whether they should start with a Limited Liability Partnership (LLP) or a One Person Company (OPC) to minimize compliance costs, with the plan of converting into a Private Limited Company (Pvt Ltd) when they raise funding. Your advice must be unequivocal: if external venture capital, angel funding, or employee stock options (ESOPs) are contemplated within 12 to 24 months, starting as anything other than a Private Limited company creates unnecessary expense, legal friction, and due diligence hurdles.

### Entity Comparison
1. **Private Limited Company (Companies Act, 2013):**
   - *Capital Structure:* Separates ownership from management. Permits issuance of equity shares, differential voting rights (DVRs), and Compulsorily Convertible Preference Shares (CCPS).
   - *ESOPs:* Well-defined legal framework under Section 62(1)(b) of the Companies Act, 2013.
   - *Taxation:* Base corporate tax rate of 22% (effective rate 25.17% inclusive of surcharge and cess under Section 115BAA of the Income-tax Act, 1961 / corresponding provisions of the Income-tax Act, 2025). Dividends are taxable in the hands of shareholders.

2. **Limited Liability Partnership (LLP Act, 2008):**
   - *Structure:* Hybrid of partnership and body corporate. Internal operations governed by an LLP Agreement.
   - *Taxation:* Flat tax rate of 30% (plus surcharge and cess). Alternate Minimum Tax (AMT) applies. Share of profit is exempt in the hands of partners under Section 10(2a) of the 1961 Act.
   - *Fundraising limitations:* LLPs cannot issue shares, debentures, or CCPS. Foreign Direct Investment (FDI) in LLPs is restricted to sectors where 100% FDI is permitted under the automatic route without performance conditions; foreign investors cannot subscribe to hybrid instruments.

3. **One Person Company (OPC):**
   - *Structure:* Introduced to allow single entrepreneurs to operate with limited liability.
   - *Fundraising limitations:* Only one natural person can be a member. Raising equity from an angel or VC requires immediate conversion into a private limited company under the Companies Act, rendering the structure redundant for scalable ventures.

### Investor Preferences and Holding Structures
Institutional venture capital funds (SEBI-registered AIFs, foreign VCs) invest almost exclusively in Private Limited entities because standard investor protection terms—liquidation preference, anti-dilution, information rights, drag-along, and tag-along rights—can only be contracted and enforced through Articles of Association (AoA) and share subscription agreements. 

For cross-border models, founders sometimes propose a foreign holding company (e.g., Singapore or Delaware) with an Indian operating subsidiary. You must advise founders that while a foreign holding company eases access to global capital, establishing one when founders reside in India requires compliance with the Foreign Exchange Management (Overseas Investment) Rules and Regulations, 2022 (FEMA OI framework), Round Tripping restrictions, and Place of Effective Management (POEM) tax exposure. Unless foreign investors explicitly make an offshore holdco a condition precedent for a multi-million-dollar cheque, an Indian Private Limited holding company is the cleanest starting point.

## 4. Worked example or checklist

| Feature | Private Limited Company | Limited Liability Partnership (LLP) | One Person Company (OPC) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Minimum Members** | 2 | 2 | 1 (natural person) |
| **Institutional Equity (VC/Angel)** | Native fit (CCPS, Equity) | Not supported | Not supported (requires conversion) |
| **ESOP Suitability** | Native fit (Section 62) | Complex/unsupported | Not supported |
| **FDI Flexibility** | High (Automatic & Approval; Equity/CCPS) | Limited (Automatic 100% only; capital only) | Prohibited for non-residents |
| **Statutory Audit** | Mandatory every year | Mandatory only if turnover > ₹40L or capital > ₹25L | Mandatory every year |
| **Base Tax Rate** | 22% (Sec 115BAA) | 30% | 22% (Sec 115BAA) |
| **Conversion Friction** | Low (baseline standard) | High (stamp duty, tax exposure) | Moderate (Companies Act conversion) |

## 5. Watch-outs
- **Conversion Traps (Section 47):** Converting an LLP to a Private Limited company under Chapter XXI of the Companies Act, 2013 is exempt from capital gains under Section 47(xiii) of the Income-tax Act, 1961 only if all assets and liabilities are transferred and partners receive shares in proportion to their capital accounts with aggregate voting rights of at least 50% for 5 years. Any deviation triggers capital gains tax on the transfer.
- **Section 42 Private Placement:** Founders often issue shares to friends and family without passing special resolutions or opening a separate bank account, violating private placement rules under Section 42 of the Companies Act, 2013, which carries severe penalties up to the amount involved or ₹2 crore.
- **FDI in LLPs:** If an LLP takes foreign investment, it cannot operate in sectors requiring government approval or having agricultural/real estate activities, and foreign investors cannot take convertible notes or preference capital.

## 7. Quick quiz
1. Can an LLP issue Compulsorily Convertible Preference Shares (CCPS) to a seed investor?
2. What is the statutory audit exemption threshold for an LLP under the LLP Act, 2008?
3. Under the Income-tax Act, 1961, what is the base corporate tax rate available to a domestic Private Limited company under Section 115BAA (excluding surcharge and cess)?

*Answers:*
1. No. An LLP has no concept of share capital or preference shares; contributions are made directly as partner capital.
2. Turnover not exceeding ₹40 lakh or capital contribution not exceeding ₹25 lakh in a financial year.
3. 22% (effective rate is 25.17% with 10% surcharge and 4% cess).

## 8. Digital Rupee today
No official RBI press releases or operational data updates on the Digital Rupee (e₹) were published in the last 30 days (unverified — check rbi.org.in). 

Regarding CBDC architecture, the RBI's October 2022 Concept Note on Central Bank Digital Currency outlined four core design choices:
1. **Type:** Issuance of Wholesale (e₹-W) for interbank settlement and Retail (e₹-R) for public use.
2. **Model:** A two-tier indirect model where the RBI issues e₹ to licensed commercial banks, and banks distribute it to end-users via digital wallets, preserving the banking sector's intermediation role.
3. **Form:** Token-based retail currency where the holder of the token owns the liability (akin to cash), ensuring peer-to-peer finality.
4. **Remuneration:** Non-interest-bearing to prevent flight of retail bank deposits to central bank liability.

For a CA advising tech startups, understanding this two-tier model is essential as fintech clients begin integrating programmable retail CBDC wallets directly into merchant checkouts.
