# Startup Advisory — Day 5: Startup Tax Holiday, Loss Carry-Forward & ESOP Deferral

_2026-11-18_

## 1. Today in one line
DPIIT recognition alone does not grant 100% tax holidays or ESOP tax deferral—Inter-Ministerial Board (IMB) certificate is mandatory under the Income-tax framework.

## 2. The concept
Founders frequently confuse DPIIT Recognition with Inter-Ministerial Board (IMB) Certification. DPIIT registration is a simple online process for self-declaration of startup status. IMB approval, however, requires explicit validation of innovation, scalability, and high growth potential by a panel of government officials and experts.

### Tax Holiday Provisions (Section 80-IAC of 1961 Act / Income-tax Act, 2025 equivalent)
An eligible startup (incorporated as a Private Limited Company or LLP between 1 April 2016 and 31 March 2025/extended dates) with IMB certification can claim a deduction of 100% of its profits for any 3 consecutive assessment years out of 10 years from incorporation. 
- The total turnover must not exceed ₹100 crore in any of the previous years for which the deduction is claimed.
- The business must not be formed by splitting up or reconstructing an existing business.
- *Act Mapping Note:* Under the Income-tax Act, 2025 (effective 1 April 2026), tax holiday deductions for eligible startups are re-codified under simplified profit-linked deduction schedules (unverified section numbering — check the official 2025 Act text).

### Carry-Forward of Losses (Section 79 of 1961 Act / Income-tax Act, 2025 equivalent)
Under general corporate tax provisions (Section 79), if shareholding carrying more than 49% voting power changes in a private company, accumulated business losses lapse and cannot be carried forward. 

For eligible DPIIT-recognized startups, a major relaxation exists:
- Business losses can be carried forward even if equity dilution exceeds 49% due to venture capital rounds, provided that *100% of the shareholders holding voting power on the last day of the year in which the loss was incurred continue to hold those shares* on the last day of the previous year in which the set-off is claimed.
- Alternatively, the standard 51% continuity rule can also be satisfied.

### ESOP Tax Deferral (Section 192(1C) & Section 17(2)(vi) of 1961 Act / Income-tax Act, 2025 equivalent)
Normally, when employees exercise ESOPs, the difference between the Fair Market Value (FMV) and exercise price is taxed as a perquisite in the hands of the employee on the date of allotment, requiring the employer to deduct TDS immediately.

For employees of **IMB-approved eligible startups**, TDS/tax payment on ESOP perquisite is deferred to the **earliest** of the following three dates:
1. Expiry of 48 months from the end of the relevant assessment year;
2. Date of sale of such ESOP shares by the employee;
3. Date on which the employee ceases to be an employee of the startup.

## 5. Watch-outs
1. **Assuming DPIIT = 100% Tax Free:** Advising a client to stop paying advance tax or claiming 80-IAC deduction based solely on DPIIT registration will result in heavy tax demands, mandatory interest (u/s 234B/C), and potential penalty.
2. **Loss of ESOP Deferral on Resignation:** HR departments often fail to deduct deferred TDS when an employee resigns. The tax becomes payable immediately upon cessation of employment.
3. **IMB Application Rejections:** Over 90% of IMB applications are rejected due to standard, non-innovative write-ups. The write-up must demonstrate high potential for employment generation or wealth creation along with patented/technological novelty.

## 8. Digital Rupee today
**News Update:** No new RBI press releases or data on the central bank digital currency (e₹) have been published in the last 30 days as of 18 November 2026.

**Concept: Two-tier distribution through banks**
The RBI operates a two-tier architectural model for the Digital Rupee (e₹). In Tier 1, the RBI issues digital currency tokens to authorized commercial banks (the primary tier). In Tier 2, commercial banks distribute e₹ to retail and corporate end-users through specialized digital wallets (the secondary tier). The RBI retains central ledger authority over token creation and total money supply, while commercial banks manage user onboarding, Know Your Customer (KYC) compliance, and customer service without putting commercial bank credit risk into the token itself.

**Why it matters to CAs and Startups:** It allows immediate finality of settlement in high-value B2B vendor payments and escrow releases without clearing house delays or intermediary credit risk.
