# Startup Advisory — Day 9: Startup Compliance Calendar

_2026-11-22_

## 1. Today in one line
A unified compliance calendar turns fragmented statutory deadlines into a high-retention annual retainer while shielding founders from crippling penalties, director disqualification, and loss of DPIIT status.

## 2. The concept
Founders rarely ignore compliance deliberately; they simply lack a consolidated view across regulatory bodies. A startup registered as a Private Limited Company falls under five distinct compliance tracks simultaneously:

### A. Ministry of Corporate Affairs (ROC)
1. **Board Meetings:** First meeting within 30 days of incorporation; minimum 4 meetings annually (or at least 1 in each half of a calendar year with a minimum 90-day gap for small companies and recognized startups under Section 173(5) of the Companies Act, 2013).
2. **Form DPT-3:** Due annually by 30 June for reporting outstanding receipt of money or loans not considered deposits (including founder loans and convertible notes).
3. **DIR-3 KYC / Web KYC:** Due annually by 30 September for every person holding an active DIN.
4. **Annual General Meeting (AGM):** Held within 6 months from the end of the financial year (by 30 September, except for the first AGM which can be within 9 months).
5. **Form AOC-4 / AOC-4 XBRL:** Due within 30 days of the AGM.
6. **Form MGT-7 / MGT-7A:** Due within 60 days of the AGM (MGT-7A applies to small companies).
7. **Form MSME-1:** Half-yearly return due by 30 April (for October–March) and 31 October (for April–September) detailing payments to micro and small enterprises delayed beyond 45 days.

### B. Direct Taxes
1. **TDS/TCS Payments:** Due by the 7th of the following month (30 April for March).
2. **TDS Quarterly Returns:** Form 24Q (salaries), 26Q (non-salaries), 27Q (foreign payments) due by 31 July, 31 October, 31 January, and 31 May.
3. **Advance Tax:** Due in four instalments: 15 June (15%), 15 September (45%), 15 December (75%), and 15 March (100%).
4. **Income Tax Returns (ITR-6):** Due by 31 October if subject to tax audit, or 31 July if no audit applies. Note: Under the Income-tax Act, 2025 (effective 1 April 2026), procedural timelines align with the modernized assessment provisions replacing the Income-tax Act, 1961 equivalents.

### C. Indirect Taxes (GST)
1. **Monthly Filers:** GSTR-1 by the 11th; GSTR-3B by the 20th of the following month.
2. **QRMP Scheme:** For eligible taxpayers, invoice furnishing facility (IFF) monthly by the 13th, GSTR-1 and GSTR-3B quarterly by the 13th and 22nd/24th respectively.
3. **Annual Return (GSTR-9 / 9C):** Due by 31 December following the end of the financial year.

### D. Labour Law & Payroll
1. **PF & ESI Deposits:** Due by the 15th of the following month.
2. **DPIIT Labour Law Self-Certification:** Under Ministry of Labour notifications, DPIIT-recognized startups can self-certify compliance for up to 5 years (via the Shram Suvidha portal) across 6 core central labour statutes (such as the Payment of Gratuity Act, Contract Labour Act, EPF, and ESI). No inspections are conducted without written permission from an officer at least one level above the inspecting officer.

### E. DPIIT Annual Governance
Maintaining startup recognition requires tracking:
1. Turnover ceiling (must remain under INR 100 crore in any previous financial year).
2. Age ceiling (under 10 years from incorporation).
3. Intimations on the Startup India portal regarding material changes in shareholding or line of business.

## 5. Watch-outs
1. **Treating Director Loans as Unregulated:** Startups frequently take unsecured loans from directors or relative shareholders. Failing to report these as exempted deposits in Form DPT-3 leads to severe penalties under Section 73/76.
2. **DIN Deactivation at Funding Time:** If a founder forgets Web-based DIR-3 KYC, their DIN gets deactivated on 1 October. If an investor issues a term sheet in November, the company cannot execute Board resolutions or file ROC forms until the DIN is restored.
3. **The Section 43B(h) MSME Exposure:** Delayed payments to micro and small enterprises require both MSME-1 filing and tax disallowance under the Income-tax Act. Ensure the startup’s accounts payables ledger flags MSME vendor registration status.
4. **Assuming DPIIT Exemption Means Zero Compliance:** Founders often mistakenly believe DPIIT recognition waives tax deductions, PF registrations, or ROC filings. DPIIT provides specific inspection relaxations and tax holiday eligibility (Section 80-IAC under the 1961 Act / codified equivalent under 2025 Act), not blanket filing immunity.

**Concept — Programmable e₹:** 
Programmable e₹ refers to digital currency embedded with smart contract logic that dictates how, where, or by whom the money can be spent. Unlike traditional bank account transfers or generic digital cash, programmable e₹ allows the payer (e.g., an enterprise or government agency) to restrict the currency's validity to a specific merchant category, geographical location, or time frame.

**Why it matters to a CA or startup client:** 
Startups can disburse non-salary allowances, vendor advances, or grant money using programmable tokens that automatically expire or restrict usage to approved business vendors, eliminating employee expense fraud and automating invoice reconciliation for tax audits.
